Plant Look Board

The plants look the same after every filter change because the problem is their shape. Every bean leaf is held at one angle, so the plant reads as a little fir tree. Here are four mockups rendered in our own viewer, what games and botanical art teach, and what to build first.

What each mockup did

clear win

Leaf pose

Young leaves stand up, mature ones spread, old ones droop below level, and each leaf gets a small random twist from its id (yaw up to 31°). The candelabra becomes a rounded bush. This is a render-only stand-in. The real fix is in the sim, where PETIOLE_ELEVATION is 0.5 rad for every leaf, so the light lens stays honest.

clear win

Paper "plate" view

The garden is swapped for a seamless paper sweep. This is the most visibly different picture of the four, and it is what a botanical plate does: the plant and its roots are the only subject. It would make a strong screenshot or plate mode, and a good base for the ink-line look.

helps

Framing and background

A narrower lens (0.42 rad), a lower camera, a muted lawn, and no tree ring. The plants now lead the frame. But the two pots still take a lot of the frame, so a hero camera on one plant matters more than any setting change.

subtle

Canopy shading from the shade signal

Leaves deep in the canopy go darker and cooler, outer leaves lighter and warmer. The effect is true but hard to see on a 25-leaf plant. It will matter more under cel bands and on dense plots.

What to build, in order

  1. Leaf elevation by age, in the sim. Young leaves near 1.2 rad, mature ones near 0.5, old lower leaves just below level. Plus a petiole twist of ±30° so the two leaf ranks don't sit in one plane. This is the single biggest change. Bean leaves really are distichous (180°), which is exactly why they look flat today without the twist.
  2. A hero camera that frames one plant's canopy at 50–60% of frame height, and a quieter background: muted lawn, no faceted trees, or the paper sweep.
  3. The plate view as a screenshot mode: paper sweep, ink lines, a scale bar, and the root system below.
  4. Canopy shading and dome normals together with the cel pass, so bands fall across the whole bush instead of flickering leaf by leaf.
  5. Live leaf motion: leaflets edge-on to a hot noon sun and folded down at night. Both are real bean behaviors, and both make the day cycle visible on the plant itself.
  6. Per-plant variation in rows, so a trellis of pole beans stops looking like wallpaper.

Elevation numbers are illustrative, not measured. A deeper source on bean leaf angle by node would firm them up before the sim change.

What games and plant art teach

Dorfromantik

Check palettes in greyscale first: readability comes from value, then hue adds mood. Each asset must read by silhouette from the game camera. They also limit vertex colours to avoid visual noise.

80.lv

Alba: A Wildlife Adventure

Soft, continuous forms come from normals transferred from a smooth proxy mesh, with gradients in place of textures. The palette came from location photos and Sorolla paintings.

ustwo games

Stylized tree breakdowns

Bend foliage normals toward a sphere so a canopy shades as one volume. Darken the interior and give each leaf its own colour offset. "Always mess with the normals."

ArtStation

Meadows (UE4 study)

Canopies are cards over sculpted spheres with the sphere's normals. Sharpening the shadow filter gives a near cel look with no post pass. Large-scale colour variation stays subtle.

80.lv

Sable

Flat shading needs lines, fog and cast shadows to rebuild depth. Outlines mark overlapping forms and fade with distance so nothing pops in.

Game Developer

Moebius-style post process

Sobel edges on depth (weighted about 25×) plus normals, a thickness offset, and low-frequency UV wobble for a hand-drawn line. Leaf-over-leaf edges need an id buffer on top of this.

maximeheckel.com

Viridi

A potted plant as the whole stage: flat, strong pastel colours, gentle outlines, a slow orbiting camera and silhouetted backdrops. It's the closest game to our single-pot view.

Kill Screen

Botany Manor

Every invented plant starts from a real one. Ours are real, so true botanical details are the art: pulvini, heart-shaped first leaves, pink nodules, pale undersides.

Journal of Geek Studies

Tiny Glade, Cloud Gardens, Equilinox, The Garden Path

Cozy builders use muted earthy palettes with one saturated subject, and growth you can watch. Tiny Glade started on Bevy and outgrew its renderer for prettier procedural output. These sources are thin on technique.

80.lv

Breath of the Wild

Stylized for readability, yet plausible and "information-dense": the stylization suggests real physics. That's a fair brief for a science sim with an illustrated look.

Zelda Universe

Botanical drawing practice

Map habit, axes, nodes and attachments before outlines or veins. Don't regularize intervals. Use fewer veins, not equal dark veins everywhere. Scale bars, not "life size".

thehistoryofart.org

Bean leaf movement

In common bean, higher light and temperature raise the leaflets on their pulvini, and drier soil makes it stronger (Yu & Berg 1994). Runner bean leaves drop at dusk and rise before dawn on a circadian clock, and its shoots circle about every 90 minutes.
