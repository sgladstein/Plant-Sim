# M2 growth forms: pole (climbing, indeterminate) common bean and a temperate C3 grass

Tags: [F] read this session (abstract, page or file); [K, to verify] prior knowledge not re-read; [I] inference. "Abstract-only" means only the abstract was read. Bush-bean baselines are from /mnt/project-files/research/plant-simulation-science-review.md (the first-round review).

## Q1. How does a pole bean climb? Mechanism, handedness, circumnutation, support limits, twining geometry

### Takeaway
Common bean has **no tendrils**. It is a **stem twiner**. The young apical internodes circumnutate, sweeping the tip in a circle about every **~1.5–2 h** (Darwin measured 1 h 57 min; another study found 80 min at 25 °C with Q10 ≈ 2). When the shoot touches a support it twines around it, in Darwin's words "**against the sun**". Seen from above in the northern hemisphere that is anticlockwise, which makes a right-handed helix in the screw sense. Success falls as the support gets thicker. Darwin's Phaseolus twined round a 3–4 inch (7.6–10 cm) post in the open air but failed on a **9 inch (≈23 cm)** one. The plan's "tendril module that searches a cone, attaches and wraps" should become a **circumnutation-sweep + contact + helical-wrap module**. Its parameters are sweep period, sweep radius, the length of the revolving zone, and a maximum support diameter.

### Cited Findings
- **Darwin [F]:** *Phaseolus vulgaris* in a greenhouse "moves against the sun". Three revolutions took 2 h 0 min, 1 h 55 min and 1 h 55 min (mean 1 h 57 min). Cut shoots in water revolved more slowly. "Most of the species of Phaseolus are twiners." Darwin, C. (1875) *The Movements and Habits of Climbing Plants*, 2nd ed. — [Project Gutenberg](https://gutenberg.org/files/2485/2485-h/2485-h.htm)
- **Darwin [F]:** for twiners in general, "the majority … ascend their supports from left to right". In healthy twiners **two internodes revolve at once**. The first 2–3 or more internodes above the cotyledons do not revolve. In the hop, three internodes revolved at once. — [Gutenberg](https://gutenberg.org/files/2485/2485-h/2485-h.htm)
- **Darwin, citing Mohl [F]:** with light from one side, *Phaseolus multiflorus* could not twine round sticks 3–4 inches across. In the open air it did, "but failed in twining round one 9 inches in diameter". "Most twining plants are adapted to ascend supports of moderate though of different thicknesses." Kidney beans climbed smooth iron and glass rods ⅓ inch (8.5 mm) across. — [Gutenberg](https://gutenberg.org/files/2485/2485-h/2485-h.htm)
- **Darwin [F]:** some *P. multiflorus* varieties make both upright thick shoots and thin twining shoots. "Fulmer's dwarf forcing-bean" sometimes made one long twining shoot. In other words, dwarf habit is not absolute. — [Gutenberg](https://gutenberg.org/files/2485/2485-h/2485-h.htm)
- **Period and temperature [F, abstract-only]:** in pole beans, shoot circumnutation and ultradian leaf movement share a **period of 80 min at 25 °C, Q10 ≈ 2**. The circadian leaf movement is temperature-compensated (Q10 ≈ 1). Millet, B. et al. (1988) *Chronobiology International*. — [Consensus](https://consensus.app/papers/details/8166f99538825c0cbdfc71cf20b1531a/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- **Period, 'Kentucky Wonder' [F, abstract-only]:** control plants circumnutated with mean periods of **124.2 min and 132.1 min** in two experiments. (The paper tests acupuncture; use only its control values.) Hou, T. et al. (1997) *Am. J. Chinese Medicine*. — [Consensus](https://consensus.app/papers/details/b7f09d9a92d15f1a8339d942c7653b5a/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- **Amplitude, soybean as a proxy [F, abstract-only]:** shoot tips moved **counterclockwise** in both species. Mean period was 108–124 min (*G. soja*) and 112–133 min (*G. max*). Amplitudes were **0.12–1.43 cm in *G. max*** (no distinct bending zone) and **2.9–8.7 cm in *G. soja*** (a twining wild type with a bending zone). Handling lengthened the period by 15%. Sothern, R. (1998) *Chronobiology International*. — [Consensus](https://consensus.app/papers/details/27410cc5c5f953bd884bc593ab6a638b/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- **Bending zone, adzuki bean epicotyl as a proxy [F, abstract-only]:** the bending zone sits **1–3 cm below the tip**, ending where elongation is complete. The period was 60 min and the rotation clockwise in that system. Iida et al. (2017) *J. Plant Research*. — [Consensus](https://consensus.app/papers/details/c01e658bd3665907ac5754ed24f54ac9/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- **Support thickness [F, abstract-only]:** five herbaceous stem twiners, *P. vulgaris* among them, were tested. **Twining success rate fell and stem diameter rose as support thickness increased.** Helix curvature β was not constant; it fell with greater stem or support diameter. More than 5 °C lower temperature raised β and thinned the stem but did not lower the success rate. 50% shade lowered success in *Ipomoea* (bean was not separately reported in the abstract). On 45° supports the lower half-turns were 40–72% more curved (in *I. triloba*). Hu, L. et al. (2017) *Ecology and Evolution* 7. — [Consensus](https://consensus.app/papers/details/2985be467df0565c925b0390158831fb/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- **Support search changes the nutation pattern [F, abstract-only]:** bean nutation dynamics differ with and without a nearby support. Raja, V. et al. (2020) *Scientific Reports*. — [Consensus](https://consensus.app/papers/details/d8fe8535fb755e9a87f56b6b2d641b52/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- **Attachment decision [F, abstract-only, preprint]:** a circumnutating bean shoot behaves like a rotating cantilever. Twining starts only once a **critical torque threshold** is reached, and twining time scales inversely with circumnutation rate. Ohad, A. et al. (2026) bioRxiv; also Ohad et al. (2025) on force measurement in *P. vulgaris*. — [Consensus 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/8d8f7513ae5a5779b359012ce5f1b1dd/?utm_source=claude_desktop); [Consensus 2025](https://consensus.app/papers/details/6a8d5cb0d3e85ff89fe7f572572cfd08/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- **What holds the coil [F, abstract-only]:** gelatinous (G) fibres are **absent from actively circumnutating internodes**. They form after an internode has stopped elongating, on the concave side of coils, and stabilise the helix against slipping. Shrub (bush) phenotypes form them in an alternating pattern for upright posture. Common bean habit can be shifted by light conditions. Onyenedum, J.G. et al. (2025) *Am. J. Botany*. — [Consensus](https://consensus.app/papers/details/6a88ee9c33395901940ef5e905eac5c7/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- **Twining geometry in an existing bean FSPM [F]:** Rutjens et al. 2025 builds the twined stem as a polygon with n = max(3, round(π / arctan(L cos η / 2R))) sides and turn angle 360°/n. L is internode length, R the pole radius and η the **helix inclination, 35–75°**, with ±10° random noise per internode above rank 1. Beyond a distance threshold (value not given) the apex grows toward the nearest pole or maize internode by positional tropism. Collisions are resolved by testing fallback angles from max(α, 90°) down to −90° in 1° steps. — [Rutjens et al. 2025, edepot.wur.nl](https://edepot.wur.nl/699776)

### Inferences
- [I] **Handedness.** "Against the sun" in the northern hemisphere means anticlockwise seen from above. Ascending anticlockwise traces a right-handed helix in the screw and physics sense (Z-helix). The botanical literature uses "dextrorse" and "sinistrorse" inconsistently, so the model card should **state the convention in words**: "bean twines anticlockwise when viewed from above, a right-handed helix". The soybean abstract's "counterclockwise" circumnutation agrees. [K, to verify] Edwards et al. (2005, *J. Ecology*) found 92% of 1,485 twining species twine right-handed this way, independent of hemisphere. Bean handedness is genetically fixed and does not mirror in the southern hemisphere [K, to verify].
- [I] **Module parameters to replace "tendril cone"**:
  - Revolving zone: the two youngest elongating internodes, about the top **10–30 cm**. (Darwin: two internodes; bending zone 1–3 cm in an adzuki epicotyl, much longer in twiners.)
  - Sweep period: **~80–130 min at 20–25 °C**, scaled by Q10 2. At a daily step the tip makes ~11–18 sweeps per day, so reaching a support can be treated as instant within the sweep radius.
  - Sweep radius: **~3–10 cm**, based on *G. soja*'s 2.9–8.7 cm and the length of the revolving zone. No direct bean value was found.
  - Capture rule: a support whose axis comes within the sweep radius of the tip gets wrapped, provided its diameter is ≤ D_max. Use success probability 1.0 for 0.5–5 cm, falling linearly to 0 at about **10–20 cm** (Darwin: OK at 7.6–10 cm in the open, fail at 23 cm).
  - Wrap geometry: Rutjens's polygon with helix inclination ~**50–60°** from horizontal [I midpoint of 35–75°]. The pitch is then p = 2πR·tan η, about 5–6 cm per turn on a 1.5 cm stake [I].
  - Coil lock-in: an internode is locked once it stops elongating, which is when G-fibres form.
- [I] Without support, the stem circumnutates, fails to find anything, droops under its own weight and lies prostrate. This matches the CIAT Type III description. The plan's "flops and shades itself" penalty can stay emergent. Node production does not need to change.

### Gaps
- No bean-specific circumnutation **amplitude or sweep radius** was found. The Raja 2020 and Guerra papers were read only in abstract, and the Guerra papers found are on pea, not bean. The Raja 2020 full text likely has tip trajectories.
- Not re-read this session: Isnard & Silk 2009 (*Am. J. Bot.* "Moving with climbing plants from Charles Darwin's time into the 21st century"), Silk & Hubbard 1991 (*J. Plant Growth Regul.*, axial forces and normal distributed loads in twining stems of morning glory), and Gianoli 2015 (climbing-plant ecology review). They are [K, to verify] background only.
- Squeeze force and minimum support diameter for bean were not found. Ohad et al. has forces; the full text was not read.

## Q2. Pole-bean development and yield parameters (CIAT habits, DSSAT, Rutjens FSPM, cultivars), versus bush bean

### Takeaway
A pole bean is CIAT **Type IV**: vegetative terminal bud, long or very long guide, twining with strong climbing ability, and pods along the stem or in the upper part. DSSAT CROPGRO separates indeterminate types only through **FL-VS = 11 PTD**: the main stem keeps making leaves for 11 photothermal days after first flower. It does **not** change TRIFL (0.34 leaves per thermal day) or RHGHT (1.0), so DSSAT has no "taller climber" parameter. Andean Habit 4 also flowers later (EM-FL 34 versus 23.5–29 PTD for Type I) and keeps expanding leaves far longer after flowering (FL-LF 34 versus 10). The 2025 Rutjens climbing-bean FSPM uses phyllochron 50–75 °C·d, base 8–14 °C, maximum internode 5–15 cm with later ranks longer, and LMA 2.25–6.75 mg cm⁻². Garden pole cultivars reach 1.5–2.1 m ('Kentucky Wonder', 67 d to first harvest).

### Inferences
- [I] **Differences from the bush-bean file** (the habit locus flip plus parameter deltas):
  - Terminal bud stays vegetative.
  - Main stem keeps initiating nodes after R1 for FL-VS 11 PTD in CROPGRO terms. For a garden pole bean, keep it initiating until the end of the season or a node cap of about 25–35. A 2 m stem at about 7–10 cm per internode is roughly 20–30 nodes [I].
  - EM-FL rises from ~25–28 to **~30–34 PTD**.
  - FL-LF rises from 10–16 to **~22–34 PTD**.
  - SD-PM rises from ~19–21 to **~22–25 PTD**.
  - PPSEN about 0.05 h⁻¹. This matters little in a 12–16 h garden photoperiod.
  - Keep TRIFL at 0.34 (phyllochron ~53 °C·d above base 7), SIZLF 133 and SLA ~320.
- [I] **Internodes.** The bush-bean review scaled YVSHT by 0.7 for a 35 cm Type I. For Type IV, use YVSHT unscaled to about node 10, then an extended tail of **~10–15 cm per internode** (Rutjens Lmax up to 0.15 m) instead of YVSHT's decline to 2.5 cm by V40. CROPGRO is a canopy model and its height table was never intended for twining climbers.
- [I] **WP22 feasibility check (2 m by day 60):**
  - Garden mean of 20 °C is ~13 °C·d per day above base 7, so the stem makes ~0.24 nodes d⁻¹.
  - Emergence takes ~5 d, so by day 60 the main stem has ~13–14 nodes.
  - Reaching 2 m therefore needs a mean internode of **~14–15 cm**. That is at the top of Rutjens's range.
  - Options: (a) 'Kentucky Wonder' tops out at 1.5–2.1 m in ~67 d, so "fills a 2 m trellis by day 60" is plausible only with warm conditions (≥22 °C mean) or by counting branches climbing too; (b) relax the target to "≥1.5 m by day 60 and 2 m by day 75".
  - Recommend the owner pick one in WP22.
- [I] Pole snap yield per plant: 8 lb per 10 ft from 24–40 plants is about 3.6 kg / 24–40, or **~90–150 g fresh pods per plant**. That is a ballpark for the trellised case only.

## Q3. Yield penalty when a climbing bean is unstaked

### Takeaway
No controlled staked-versus-unstaked trial with numbers was read this session. The available numbers are an extension claim of **50–90% yield loss with inadequate staking**, with no study cited, and a farm survey from Rwanda where yield correlated with stake density (r = 0.73; ~1.1 t ha⁻¹ at ~18,000 stakes ha⁻¹ versus ~2.0–2.3 t ha⁻¹ at 22,000–25,000).

### Cited Findings
- Inadequate staking causes a yield loss of "50% to 90%". Highest yields come at ≥20,000 stakes ha⁻¹, with gains continuing up to 50,000. A string trellis at 16,667 stakes ha⁻¹ gave an "insignificant yield reduction". The page cites no primary study. — [IITA PROPAS](https://propas.iita.org/en/solutions/low-cost-staking-of-beans/95/details/)
- Northern Rwanda, 12 farms and 23 fields: stake density against grain yield gave very resource poor 18,200 ha⁻¹ → 1.18 t ha⁻¹; resource poor 18,900 → 1.14; resource rich 24,800 → 2.02; very resource rich 22,000 → 2.27. Across 15,000–35,000 stakes ha⁻¹, r = 0.73. Stake height also correlated with grain and biomass yield. Reckling, M., Franke, A.C., Vanlauwe, B., Kantengwa, S., Giller, K.E. (2011) Tropentag poster. — [tropentag.de](https://www.tropentag.de/2011/abstracts/posters/925.pdf)
- A Ugandan survey covered stake types only (eucalyptus 92.7% in Kabale; elephant grass 62% in Kisoro) and gave no yields. Takusewanya et al. (2017). — [UNCST repository](https://nru.uncst.go.ug/items/fafabf0c-6b2b-484c-bd97-44be849844c8)
- Climbers that find support outperform prostrate ones (a general statement in the introduction of a pea study). — [Wang et al. 2023, Plants, abstract](https://consensus.app/papers/details/b97888235a915f69b26720c6b862ea43/?utm_source=claude_desktop)

### Inferences
- [I] For WP22's "without a trellis it flops and yields less", use an emergent penalty with an expected band of **−30% to −70%** grain or pod yield relative to trellised. The lower bound comes from the Rwanda contrast at moderate stake density; the upper is inside the 50–90% extension claim. Mechanisms: self-shading of prostrate leaves, pods touching soil (rot, if modelled), and lower light interception per leaf area.

### Gaps
- No controlled experiment comparing unstaked and staked Type IV yield (CIAT 1970s–80s trials, e.g., Davis & García or Graham & Rosas) was found. A follow-up search on CIAT climbing-bean staking trials is needed. Older CIAT annual reports likely have the numbers.

## Q4. Which temperate C3 grass to model, and its parameters

### Takeaway
Recommend **spring wheat (*Triticum aestivum*)** for WP23. The grass module can be reused for perennial ryegrass later by changing parameters.
- **Data:** wheat has the most citable parameters. ADEL-wheat, CN-Wheat and elong-Wheat (open source, with a parameter file read this session) and WALTer give a phyllochron of ~99 °C·d, elongation durations in phyllochrons, and tillering cessation rules. APSIM, DSSAT and CPlantBox wheat files exist as well.
- **Game moments:** it gives tillers, stem elongation, heading and a harvestable head.
- **Ryegrass:** has good site-filling and R:FR tillering data (Neuteboom 1989; Gautier 1999). It is the better choice if the game wants a lawn or mowing grass. Mowing (defoliation) is its natural interaction, but that needs regrowth from reserves (LINGRA) and perenniality.

  - Structural lamina mass (SSLW): 22 g m⁻² for ranks 1–7, rising to 31 at rank 11.
  - Sheath lineic structural mass (LSSW) by rank 1–11: 0.08 → 0.65 g m⁻¹.
  - Sheath:lamina ratio from a cubic in rank (Dornbush 2011): a −0.0021, b 0.037, c −0.1527, d 0.4962.
  - Internode initiation 5 plastochrons after the leaf; internode elongation ends at te 331.8.
  - Growth stops below sucrose 240 µmol C g⁻¹.
  - Temperature response: Eyring above 9 °C (Ea/R 8900 K, ΔH/R 20735.5 K, ΔS/R 68.432), linear below.
- **Phyllochron, field winter wheat (NC) [F]:** ~117 and ~100 GDD per leaf for two sowing dates. The base temperature is not stated; it is usually 0 °C for wheat [K]. Primary sources listed: Baker et al. 1986; Klepper, Rickman & Peterson 1982 (*Agron. J.* 74:789); Krenzer & Nipp 1991; Cao & Moss 1989 (*Crop Sci.* 29:1018). — [ASA 2013 handout](https://a-c-s.confex.com/crops/2013am/webprogram/Handout/Paper79854/ASA%20Phyllochron%20Interval%20final%20revision.pdf)
- **Development thermal requirements [F]:**
  - Development stops below ~0 °C and above ~30 °C; base and optimum rise during development.
  - Emergence to double ridge takes ≥200 °C·d. Sowing to heading takes **≥760 °C·d** in a short-duration wheat at 15 h days, and about 1,500 °C·d at 10 h.
  - **Vernalization is effective at 0–12 °C.**
  - — [FAO X8234E §6 (Rawson & Gómez-Macpherson, *Irrigated Wheat*)](https://www.fao.org/4/x8234e/x8234e09.htm)
- **Grass leaf elongation model [F, abstract-only]:** the phyllochron emerges from leaf elongation and whorl construction: emergence events trigger growth-zone ontogeny. Fournier, C. et al. (2005) *New Phytologist*. — [Consensus](https://consensus.app/papers/details/faf8f696d63d526798aa1f7cc72d8f27/?utm_source=claude_desktop)

**Ryegrass tillering and leaves**
- **Site filling [F, abstract-only]:** perennial ryegrass's maximum site filling is **0.693 tillers per tiller per leaf-appearance interval**, counting the prophyll bud. Spaced plants in a climate room came close to it. Neuteboom, J. & Lantinga, E. (1989) *Annals of Botany*. — [Consensus](https://consensus.app/papers/details/98609d2af37357298bff47189425fe75/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- **Light effects on tillering [F, abstract-only]:** **low R:FR reduced tillering by reducing site filling**. Lower PPFD changed the phyllochron, and lower blue light had no effect. Defoliation lowered site filling in a long-leaved population. Gautier, H., Varlet-Grancher, C., Hazard, L. (1999) *Annals of Botany*. — [Consensus](https://consensus.app/papers/details/39e4ad4690215eb995258f3184be344b/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- **Ryegrass phyllochron and leaf elongation in the field [F, abstract-only]:** phyllochron **87.9 °C·d per leaf** (pasture brome 74.4). The longest defoliation interval gave leaf elongation rate 0.63 cm d⁻¹ and total leaf length 54.6 cm. Calvache et al. (2020) *Agronomy*. — [Consensus](https://consensus.app/papers/details/d57ff64cfe8c585399bd3b3623ecb2fc/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- **Intercalary growth zone [F, abstract-only]:** **elongation is confined to the basal 20–30 mm** of the leaf, with the maximum relative elementwise growth rate mid-zone. Epidermal cells lengthen from 12 µm to ~550 µm. Elongation rate is steady from tip emergence to the blade-to-sheath transition, then falls quickly while only the sheath grows. Schnyder, H. et al. (1990) *Planta*. — [Consensus](https://consensus.app/papers/details/dad0e443922b5adf80e10bfce806b6e6/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- **Leaf lifespan [F, abstract-only]:** the "3.0-leaf stage" equals one leaf lifespan, so a ryegrass tiller carries about 3 live leaves. Berone et al. (2008) *Grass and Forage Science*. — [Consensus](https://consensus.app/papers/details/9749e284702c5027829ae0c958cadd30/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- **Leaf length versus tillering [F, abstract-only]:** longer leaves come from higher leaf elongation rate, not longer elongation duration. Long-leaved lines had lower site filling. Bahmani et al. (2000) *Crop Science*. — [Consensus](https://consensus.app/papers/details/f84ef8ac27875d878ae3a7fcaca3a4d7/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- **Tillering drivers [F, abstract-only]:** relative tiller appearance rate = leaf appearance rate × site filling. Site filling falls with low N and with rising LAI. van Loo (1993), PhD thesis, Wageningen. — [Consensus](https://consensus.app/papers/details/50d300f918c15619a02e3c6716008e42/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- **Sink/source sward model [F, abstract-only]:** LINGRA, a ryegrass sink/source model with temperature-driven leaf elongation and tillering and remobilisation from stubble reserves. Validated at 35 European sites with 14–19% error. Schapendonk et al. (1998). — [Consensus](https://consensus.app/papers/details/36ab7637ec995386b07a6692f8f50230/?utm_source=claude_desktop)

### Inferences
- [I] **Proposed wheat parameter set for WP23.** [F] values are from the sources above; the rest need checking.
  - Phyllochron **~100 °C·d above base 0** [F WALTer 99; NC 100–117].
  - Plastochron ~45 °C·d [F].
  - Leaf blade elongates for ~1.6 phyllochrons, sheath 0.4, internode 2 [F].
  - Final main-stem leaves 8–12 (spring wheat ~8–9 [K, to verify]; winter 11.3 [F]).
  - Max leaf width 4 mm (rank 3) to 18 mm (flag) [F].
  - Final blade length by rank rises from ~8–10 cm (leaf 1) to ~25–30 cm at the upper leaves, then the flag is shorter [K, to verify; ADEL-wheat dimension tables not read]; capped at 45 cm [F].
- [I] **Tillering rule.**
  - Primary tiller T_n appears at leaf n + 2 or n + 3 on the main stem [K, to verify; Klepper et al. 1982: T1 appears in the axil of leaf 1 when leaf 4 emerges]. This is equivalent to WALTer's bud delay plus the emergence lag.
  - Each tiller then makes leaves at the main-stem phyllochron.
  - Bud outgrowth probability is site filling ≤0.69, scaled down by R:FR below ~0.5 and to zero at **R:FR ≤ 0.35** or GAI ≥ 0.5–0.6 [F thresholds].
  - Late tillers die (regress) at stem elongation when their intercepted PAR falls below a threshold [F WALTer].
  - This fits the plan's §3.4 light-signal × reserve rule for bud activation.
- [I] **Tiller-count and height ranges for the WP23 acceptance test.** All [K, to verify]:
  - Field density (~200–300 plants m⁻²): **2–5 tillers per plant** at peak and 1–3 surviving ears.
  - Spaced garden plant: **15–40+ tillers at peak**. The Darwinkel 1978 density series in WALTer is the citable source.
  - Height: semi-dwarf modern wheat **0.7–1.0 m**; tall landraces 1.2–1.5 m.
  - Ryegrass, if chosen: spaced plants reach tens to >100 tillers; sward height is set by mowing.
- [I] **Gas exchange.** [K, to verify] Wheat flag-leaf Vcmax25 ~ 90–130 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ and Jmax25 ~ 150–230. Medlyn g1 from Lin et al. 2015 (*Nature Climate Change*): C3 crops ≈ 5.8 kPa^0.5, C3 grasses ≈ 5.3 kPa^0.5. This is about the bean's 5.8 in the first-round review.
- [I] **SLA.** elong-Wheat structural lamina mass of 22 g m⁻² implies a structural-only SLA of ~450 cm² g⁻¹. Adding non-structural mass gives a total SLA of [K, to verify] ~200–300 cm² g⁻¹.
- [I] **Roots.** The fibrous system is ~3–6 seminal roots at germination (CPlantBox lumps 15 basal roots at t = 0) plus nodal (crown) roots per tiller from the coleoptile node and stem nodes as tillers appear [K, to verify].
  - Seminal elongation ~2 cm d⁻¹ [F CPlantBox 2.2], maximum depth 0.8–1.5 m.
  - No taproot.
  - The bush-bean CRootBox root-type machinery is reused with a "nodal root per new tiller node" emergence rule [I].
- [I] **Cardinal temperatures.** [K, to verify] APSIM-Wheat uses 0 / 26 / 34 °C for development thermal time; leaf appearance is linear above ~0 °C to about 25 °C. This is consistent with FAO "below ~0 °C, above ~30 °C too hot" [F].
- [I] **Vernalization and photoperiod.** Pick a **spring wheat**, so there is no vernalization requirement and a garden sowing in spring heads in ~760–1,000 °C·d [F lower bound]. It stays long-day sensitive: heading is about twice as fast at 15 h as at 10 h [F]. Model photoperiod as a multiplier on the development rate before heading.
