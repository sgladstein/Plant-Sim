# Make the pole bean twine, not tendril

Five findings should change what M2 and M3 build. First, **common bean has no tendrils**. It is a stem twiner: the shoot tip circles every 80 to 130 minutes, wraps any support it touches anticlockwise (seen from above), and fails on posts thicker than about 20 cm. WP22's "tendril module that searches a cone" should become a circumnutation, contact and helical-wrap module, and the `Tendril` organ kind should go. Second, **the WP24 nitrogen design should copy CROPGRO's demand-driven fixation**. CROPGRO-Drybean supplies every parameter it needs, from specific nodule activity to temperature, drought and flooding curves. Under that scheme the plan's "10 to 15% of assimilate" becomes an emergent check rather than a parameter. The first-round report under-priced fixation: the correct CROPGRO cost is **about 7.1 g C per g N fixed, not 2.8**. Third, **bean genetics argues for light linkage and a separate seed-colour layer**. The named loci the game wants share chromosomes: *fin* with *Ppd* on Pv01, *St* with *I* on Pv02, *P* with phaseolin and seed size on Pv07. Seed-coat colour is epistatic, with *P* acting as a white-or-coloured gate, which the additive-plus-dominance model cannot express. The plan's mutation rate of 1e-3 per locus per gamete is 100 to 1,000 times the visible-mutation rate. Fourth, **validation data for small-plot competition exist but are thin**. Clavijo Michelangeli 2019 supports the M2 density plateau but shows that the §8.3 row is mis-worded. Weed critical-period and maize–bean LER studies give good challenge targets. No open per-plant bean competition dataset was found. Fifth, **the M1 20 cm pot runs at about 3.5 g of plant per litre**, above the 2 g/L at which real plants become pot-limited. Pot runs should therefore not be compared one-for-one with field per-plant targets. Spring wheat is the better-documented choice for the WP23 grass. Six items need an owner decision; each has a recommendation in the decision section below.

## Six decisions the owner should make before the work packages start

**Decision 1: tendrils to twining (WP22, §3.3, §3.4, §11.3). Recommend twining.** Darwin timed *Phaseolus vulgaris* sweeping its tip "against the sun" in 1 h 57 min on average and called most *Phaseolus* "twiners" ([Darwin 1875, F](https://gutenberg.org/files/2485/2485-h/2485-h.htm)). Later work found an 80-minute period at 25 °C with Q10 ≈ 2 ([Millet 1988, F abstract](https://consensus.app/papers/details/8166f99538825c0cbdfc71cf20b1531a/?utm_source=claude_desktop)), and 124–132 min in 'Kentucky Wonder' controls ([Hou 1997, F abstract](https://consensus.app/papers/details/b7f09d9a92d15f1a8339d942c7653b5a/?utm_source=claude_desktop)). Tendrils belong to peas. Shipping them on a bean would be the most visible botanical error in M2, and the fix is cheaper than the plan: the `Tendril` organ disappears and the internode gains a twining state. The model card should state the handedness in words, "anticlockwise seen from above, a right-handed helix", because botanists use "dextrorse" in both senses [I]. Handedness is fixed and does not mirror in the southern hemisphere [K, to verify].

**Decision 2: WP22's "fills a 2 m trellis by day 60". Recommend ≥1.5 m by day 60 and 2 m by day 75.** The arithmetic goes as follows [I]. The CROPGRO phyllochron (TRIFL 0.34 nodes per thermal day) on the first-round 7/25 °C trapezoid gives about 0.25 nodes d⁻¹ at a 20 °C mean. After about 5 days to emergence, the main stem has about 13.5 nodes by day 60. Reaching 2 m therefore needs **≈15 cm per internode**, the upper limit of Rutjens's 5–15 cm sensitivity range ([Rutjens 2025, F](https://edepot.wur.nl/699776)). At a 22 °C mean the requirement drops to 12.8 cm, which is plausible. 'Kentucky Wonder' tops out at 1.5–2.1 m and first harvest takes 67 days ([Gurney's, F](https://gurneys.com/products/kentucky_wonder_pole_beans_)). The alternative is to keep 2 m by day 60 and specify a warm weather file (≥22 °C mean), or to count climbing branches. Do not import Rutjens's base temperature (8–14 °C, baseline 11) without its phyllochron: on base 11 a 20 °C day gives only 9 °C·d, and the stem would stall at about 8 nodes [I].

**Decision 3: linkage vs §3.12 "loci are unlinked" (WP28). Recommend adopting light linkage in WP28.** Bean has 11 chromosomes and a map of about 1,100 cM. The refined BAT93 × Jalo map is 1,097.5 cM, with linkage groups from 46.5 cM (Pv09) to 147 cM (Pv01) ([Blair 2018, F](https://escholarship.org/content/qt042809xf/qt042809xf.pdf?t=shj4cn)). The showcase loci really are linked: *fin* with *Ppd* on Pv01 ([González 2021, F](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpls.2020.599462/full)), and *P* with seed weight ([McClean 2018, F](https://www.pubmed.gov/29897103/)), with phaseolin on Pv07. Linkage costs a few dozen RNG draws per gamete. It leaves "F2 variance > F1 variance" intact. It creates a believable puzzle: recovering bush habit with day-neutral flowering from a repulsion-phase cross. Adding it now affects one model version; adding it later regenerates golden hashes a second time [I]. The cM distances between *fin* and *Ppd*, and between *P* and the seed-size QTL, were not found. Use placeholders tagged "~" (about 10–20 cM). The fallback is to keep loci unlinked in M3 and add only those two linkages in a later WP.

**Decision 4: mutation rate (WP28, §3.12, §6.1). Recommend two tiers.** At 30 loci, 1e-3 per locus per gamete puts a new mutation in about 6% of seeds [I]. Spontaneous visible-mutation rates in plants are about 1e-5 to 1e-6 per gamete [K, to verify]. A white-seeded pure line throwing coloured offspring every few generations would teach the wrong lesson. Polygenic mutational variance per generation is, however, about 1e-3 of environmental variance across organisms (Houle 1996, [K, to verify]). So 1e-3 is defensible for anonymous QTL with small effects. Recommend 1e-3 on anonymous QTL (effect SD 0.1–0.2 of allelic SD), 1e-5 on named loci, and a model-card note calling this a game approximation.

**Decision 5: the grass, spring wheat vs perennial ryegrass (WP23). Recommend spring wheat.** Wheat has the deepest open parameter base. WALTer gives phyllochron, extension durations and tillering-cessation rules ([Lecarpentier 2019, F](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6589517)). elong-Wheat gives leaf widths and elongation rates by rank ([elong-Wheat parameters, F](https://wheatfspm.readthedocs.io/en/latest/_modules/openalea/elongwheat/parameters.html)). CPlantBox ships a wheat root file ([CPlantBox wheat.xml, F](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Plant-Root-Soil-Interactions-Modelling/CPlantBox/master/modelparameter/structural/rootsystem/wheat.xml)). A spring type needs no vernalization and gives the game tillers, stem elongation, heading and a harvestable head. Ryegrass wins only if the owner wants a lawn: its natural interaction is mowing, which needs reserve-driven regrowth and perenniality (LINGRA, [Schapendonk 1998, F abstract](https://consensus.app/papers/details/36ab7637ec995386b07a6692f8f50230/?utm_source=claude_desktop)). Build the module so that ryegrass is a parameter swap later.

**Decision 6: seed-colour epistasis outside the additive model (WP28). Recommend a separate visual-trait rule table.** Recessive *pp* gives white seed and white flowers whatever the other colour genes carry. *T* gates full versus partial colour, and *C* carries tightly linked pattern modifiers ([Plestenjak 2025, F](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpls.2024.1523745/full); [McClean 2018, F](https://www.pubmed.gov/29897103/)). An additive-plus-dominance sum cannot produce a gate. Colour has no physiological effect, so a small rule table (gate, base colour, intensity, pattern) driving only `appearance` keeps the physiological genome clean. The one physiological tie is P's linkage to seed size, and chromosome positions carry it, not the table. The full epistasis table was not found; Bassett 2007 and Parker 2024 are the primary sources to read before WP28 [K, to verify].

**Smaller decision: the pot (row 1). Recommend documenting, not enlarging.** The pot keeps the M1 visuals and its run cost. The model card should say that the sim does not reproduce the 43%-per-doubling pot-volume effect ([Poorter 2012, F](https://www.esalq.usp.br/lepse/imgs/conteudo_thumb/Pot-size-matters-a-meta-analysis-of-the-effects-of-rooting.pdf)) except through water supply and wall deflection.

## Correction to the first-round report: fixation costs 7.1 g C per g N, not 2.8

The first-round report said nodule cost could be priced at "about 2.8 g C per g N for the fixation process (CROPGRO)", and its parameter table listed "about 2.8–5 g C per g N". **That figure is wrong by a factor of about 2.5.** CROPGRO-Drybean sets RFIXN = 2.830 **g CH₂O per g protein** fixed (BNGRO048.SPE line 19, [F]), and its code converts with 0.16 g N per g protein (`CUSFIX = NFIXN/0.16 × RFIXN`, [NFIX.for, F](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DSSAT/dssat-csm-os/develop/Plant/CROPGRO/NFIX.for)). So 2.83 ÷ 0.16 = 17.7 g CH₂O per g N, and × 0.40 (the carbon fraction of CH₂O) = **7.07 g C per g N** [I]. The 2.8 came from OpenSimRoot's comment. That comment says CROPGRO uses "7.06 g glucose/g N fixed" and then sets 3.95e-5 g C per µmol N = 2.82 g C per g N ([carioca.xml, F](https://gitlab.com/rootmodels/OpenSimRoot/-/raw/master/OpenSimRoot/InputFiles/plantParameters/Bean/Carioca/carioca.xml)). The 7.06 is already grams of carbon. Labelling it glucose and multiplying by 0.4 again gives 2.8 [I]. For comparison, CROPGRO's nitrate assimilation (RNO3C 2.556) is **6.4 g C per g N** [I], so in CROPGRO fixation costs only about 10% more than nitrate. That small gap is why demand-driven allocation, not cost, does most of the work of suppressing fixation in fertile soil.

Use **7.1 g C per g N** as the default with source "BNGRO048 RFIXN", and allow 4–8 in sensitivity runs. Then grow nodules (4.8% N) and pay their maintenance explicitly. The research notes suggested a rounded 6; 7.1 is preferred because it keeps the CROPGRO nodule parameter set internally consistent [I]. Minchin & Witty's 3–5 g C per g N, which includes nodule construction and maintenance, comes from the same OpenSimRoot comment. It shows the literature spread, not a better central value [K, to verify against the primary source].

## Species parameters: pole bean, bush bean and spring wheat

CROPGRO separates indeterminate from determinate beans mainly through **FL-VS** (main-stem node production continues 11 photothermal days after first flower) and later, longer phenology. Two geometry notes help the implementer. Rutjens builds the twined stem as a polygon with n = max(3, round(π / arctan(L·cos η / 2R))) sides, where L is internode length, R the support radius and η the helix inclination ([Rutjens 2025, F](https://edepot.wur.nl/699776)). The pitch is p = 2πR·tan η. On a 1.5 cm stake (R = 0.75 cm, measured to the support axis) that gives **5.6 cm per turn at 50° and 8.2 cm at 60°** [I]. The research notes' "5–6 cm" holds only at the low end. Add the stem radius to R for the stem centreline. Coils lock once an internode stops elongating, when gelatinous fibres form on the concave side ([Onyenedum 2025, F abstract](https://consensus.app/papers/details/6a88ee9c33395901940ef5e905eac5c7/?utm_source=claude_desktop)). Success falls and helix curvature drops as supports thicken ([Hu 2017, F abstract](https://consensus.app/papers/details/2985be467df0565c925b0390158831fb/?utm_source=claude_desktop)). A recent preprint models attachment as a torque threshold on a rotating cantilever ([Ohad 2026, F abstract, preprint](https://consensus.app/papers/details/8d8f7513ae5a5779b359012ce5f1b1dd/?utm_source=claude_desktop)). At a daily step a tip makes 11–18 sweeps, so capture within the sweep radius can be treated as instant [I].

## Nitrogen: CROPGRO's structure fits the game

Every established soil-N model uses the same layout: fresh organic matter, microbial and humic pools decaying first-order with temperature and water factors, and mineral NH₄ and NO₃, of which only NO₃ moves with water ([APSIM Nutrient.json, F](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/APSIMInitiative/ApsimX/master/Models/Resources/Nutrient.json)). The game needs only five states per cell. Keeping NH₄ and NO₃ separate is the one complication worth paying for: only nitrate leaches, and nitrate, not ammonium, is the main nodulation inhibitor [K, to verify]. Uptake is where the plan diverges most. At 10 mg N kg⁻¹ the soil solution holds about 3.7 mM, more than 200 times the bean nitrate Km of 16.1 µM ([OpenSimRoot nitrate.xml, F](https://gitlab.com/rootmodels/OpenSimRoot/-/raw/master/OpenSimRoot/InputFiles/plantParameters/Bean/Carioca/nitrate.xml)). Solution-concentration Michaelis–Menten is therefore saturated in every fertile cell [I]. The real limit is transport to the root, which happens at millimetre scale inside a 5 cm cell. CROPGRO's saturating function of bulk soil ppm × root length is the right coarse-grid equivalent. OpenSimRoot's surface-based Imax and CROPGRO's per-length capacity agree within about 2.5× once units are reconciled (2.5 vs 6 µg N per cm root per day) [I].

Fixation should follow CROPGRO's logic. Carbon goes to nodules only for N demand that uptake and mining leave unmet (`CTONODR = max(0, (NDMREP − NAVL) × RFIXN/0.16)`, [CROPGRO.for, F](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DSSAT/dssat-csm-os/develop/Plant/CROPGRO/CROPGRO.for)). Nitrate suppression of fixation then emerges without a separate rule. Demand-driven allocation alone still lets nodules form in rich soil, while real bean nodulation is visibly reduced. Hence the recommended explicit multiplier on nodule growth: 1 at ≤5 ppm NO₃, falling to 0.1 at 30 ppm [I, calibrate to the 2–10 mM solution thresholds, K]. Bean is the poorest grain-legume fixer. In a meta-analysis of 83 studies its Ndfa slope was 0.57 against 0.84 for other legumes ([Palmero 2022, F abstract](https://consensus.app/papers/details/b29e560b555153669b9bb2c4a8db2898/?utm_source=claude_desktop)). Across 63 genotypes Ndfa ran from 19.6% to 88.4% ([Gunununu 2026, F abstract](https://consensus.app/papers/details/5e0732a8155f5531b9688595ad137338/?utm_source=claude_desktop)), which makes fixation efficiency a natural M3 locus. Drought "severely limits" bean fixation ([Polanía 2016, F abstract](https://consensus.app/papers/details/b24dd6cf98f85feca11879c8606932fa/?utm_source=claude_desktop)), which CROPGRO's turgor curve and 8-day memory capture.

Reproduction is straightforward. Bean is an inbreeder: anthers dehisce in the closed keel, and outcrossing ran from below 0.74% in Asturias ([Ferreira 2000, F abstract](https://consensus.app/papers/details/d3ed065bd50b556c84c4bf3c6aff2a97/?utm_source=claude_desktop)) to a weighted 6.9% over about 120,000 Californian seedlings ([Ibarra-Pérez 1997, F abstract](https://consensus.app/papers/details/a1238c7e29ed57e99f510c1b32c68164/?utm_source=claude_desktop)). Glasshouse crosses gave 0.43 F1 hybrids per pollination with emasculation. Without emasculation they gave 0.62 per pollination, but with 51 selfs. The cited "hooking" method reaches 70–90% pod set and 4–6 seeds per pod ([Usher & Redden 1986, F](https://era.dpi.qld.gov.au/id/eprint/13349/1/QJAAS_43%5B2%5D_1986_pp115-117_usher%26redden.pdf)). With pure-line founders, an F1 shows only environmental variance and an F2 shows V_E + V_A + V_D, so WP28's "F2 variance exceeds F1 variance" holds by construction. Heterozygosity halves each selfing generation, giving about 94% homozygosity at F5 [I]. Selection is strong enough to satisfy the M3 exit. Selecting the top 2 of 10 (i ≈ 1.4) at h² ≈ 0.7 moves seed size about 1 σP per generation, shrinking as lines fix, so the divergent lines should be more than 3 σP apart after five generations [I]. AlphaSimR is the reference design to mirror and an offline cross-check for segregation ratios and selection response ([Roslin AlphaSimR, F](https://vet.ed.ac.uk/roslin/research/divisions/quantitative-biology/highlander-lab/software/alphasimr)).

## Validation data and tests for competition, nitrogen and breeding

No open per-plant bean competition dataset was found on Dryad, Zenodo or Dataverse, so M2 validation leans on published figures that must be digitised. **Clavijo Michelangeli 2019 is the anchor.** It tested six densities (5–35 plants m⁻²), three growth habits and two Colombian sites. LAI rose from about 1.5 to 2.5–2.8 and was flat from 20 plants m⁻². Leaf area per plant fell from more than 0.3 m² to less than 0.1 m². Leaf N per unit leaf area held at about 2.0 g m⁻² (Darién) or 1.3 g m⁻² (Palmira) at all densities ([CORE PDF, F](https://core.ac.uk/download/224976763.pdf)). It covers Types I–III only. There are no pole-bean density data and no fitted Shinozaki–Kira parameters for *Phaseolus*. Weed studies fit the critical period consistently: from about V2 or the second trifoliolate to first flower ([Woolley 1993, F abstract](https://consensus.app/papers/details/a1efbef1f95c5b9aa13a608d3af4e190/?utm_source=claude_desktop); [Beiermann 2021, F abstract](https://consensus.app/papers/details/8fbf2107033d5f24bd68e0f548c1adb5/?utm_source=claude_desktop)). Season-long losses ran 31–94% across North American regions, with a mean of 71% ([Soltani 2018, F abstract](https://consensus.app/papers/details/7f2ae89dfc885ae88ff12114a04702eb/?utm_source=claude_desktop)). Maize–bean intercrops give LER 1.34 in a milpa synthesis and 1.64–1.99 in an Egyptian relay trial where bean was the suppressed partner (partial LER 0.72–0.98) ([El-Mehy 2023, F](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2023.1052392/full)). Rutjens 2025 states that it could not validate quantitatively for lack of data. Agreement with it is a plausibility check, not validation.

## Where the plan diverges from established practice

Four divergences are errors to fix. The **tendril** is botanically wrong for *Phaseolus*. **Solution Michaelis–Menten uptake** looks mechanistic but is just saturated at 2–5 cm resolution. Every crop model that works at that scale (CROPGRO, APSIM) uses a bulk-soil saturating function with root length as the driver. The **fixed 10–15% nodule cost** reverses the causality CROPGRO encodes: cost should follow nitrogen fixed, and fixation should follow unmet demand. The **§8.3 density row** names the wrong quantity, since leaf N per leaf area is the variable Clavijo found invariant.

Three divergences are defensible game simplifications that need explicit model-card notes. **Unlinked loci** are a valid special case of a linkage map, though cheap linkage is closer to AlphaSimR, the field standard. **Mutation at 1e-3** works as aggregate polygenic mutational variance but not as a visible-locus rate. **Pot runs compared with field targets** break the biology above about 2 g/L but buy a single-plant scene. One departure is a feature: making yield an emergent consequence of physiology rather than a heritable parameter. It matches breeding data, where yield heritability (0.61) is lower and more environment-dependent than that of its components.

Three gaps remain open. No bean-specific circumnutation amplitude, Cousens I and A values, or per-plant mass distributions were found. Bean-specific nitrate thresholds for nodulation and primary nodule time courses rest on [K] recall (Streeter 1988; Piha & Munns 1987). Chromosome positions for most colour loci, and the cM distances for the showcase linkages, are unverified. Each is tagged where it appears, and none blocks a WP from starting.

## Conclusion

The M2 and M3 science is more settled than the plan's wording suggests. The main risk is copying a plausible-sounding mechanism, not missing a parameter: tendrils on a twiner, concentration kinetics on a 5 cm grid, a flat percentage for a demand-driven cost. In each case the literature already has a better mechanism of similar or lower implementation cost. CROPGRO alone covers pole phenology, uptake, nodules, fixation cost and N mining in one internally consistent, BSD-licensed set. Because the set is internally consistent, the first-round 2.8 g C per g N slip is worth correcting before WP24 calibrates against it.

The breeding game gets more than it asked for. Bean's real linkage groups and its *P* colour gate supply ready-made puzzles: a repulsion-phase habit and photoperiod cross, or a white female that reveals failed crosses. They cost a chromosome field and a small rule table. Those choices, and the five other owner decisions, are cheapest before WP22 and WP28 start, because each changes a data structure or a golden hash. Settled later, each one costs a model version bump.
